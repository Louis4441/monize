# v1.19.0

## Hideable table columns

The transaction register, the holdings table and the brokerage register can now hide the columns you don't need. On the register, hide Description, Ref #, Tags, Attachments or Status; on the holdings table, Shares, Avg Cost, Price, Cost Basis, Mkt Value, Gain/Loss or Portfolio %; on the brokerage register, Shares, Price or Status.

- **Two ways to hide a column.** Use the **Columns** menu in the table's toolbar (or beside the holdings table's heading), or long-press or right-click a column's header and choose "Hide column" from the sheet.
- **One way to bring it back.** Reopen the Columns menu, which shows a count of what's hidden, and check the column again, or use "Show all".
- **The choice follows your account**, not your browser or device -- hide Tags on your phone and it stays hidden when you sign in on a desktop.

## Advanced transaction search

The search box on the Transactions page, and the search icon in the top bar, now understand a small search language, so you can ask for exactly what you mean without regular expressions.

- **Find cheque 10, and only cheque 10:** `#10` (or `ref:=10`) matches reference number 10 and not 100, 1010, a $10.00 amount or a memo containing "10". Leading zeros are ignored, so `#10` also finds `0010`.
- **Name a field, exclude a word:** `payee:costco -cat:gas` finds Costco transactions that aren't categorized as gas. `field:text` means contains, `field:=text` means exactly, and `*` is a wildcard (`ref:10*`). The fields are payee, memo, cat, tag, account, ref, amount, date, status, is and has.
- **Ranges for amounts and dates:** `date:2026-03 amount:>100` finds anything over 100 in March 2026; `amount:50..100` and `date:>=2026-01-01` work too.
- **See how your search was read.** Under the search box, a line spells it out ("Reference is 10", "not Category contains gas"), and anything it couldn't understand is struck through. The **?** beside the box lists the syntax, and choosing an example puts it in the box.

**One change to how searches behave:** a search of several words now finds transactions containing all of those words anywhere, rather than only that exact phrase. Put the words in quotes (`"costco gas"`) to search for the phrase.

## Government bonds

### A bond engine ready for more countries

Monize now values government bonds from their terms of issue instead of a market price. This is only the engine: each country needs its own implementation (an adapter) before Monize can handle its bonds. Poland is the first country implemented.

- **Rate rules:** a fixed rate, a benchmark plus a spread (for example a central bank reference rate), inflation plus a margin, and zero-coupon discount instruments such as treasury bills.
- **Interest:** capitalised or paid as coupons, accrued per day, rounded the way the issuer rounds.
- **Early redemption:** the redemption window, the fee, and whether the fee may cut into the principal.
- **Redemption split:** what a redemption pays is split into returned principal and interest, so interest is never booked as a capital gain.
- **One country, one adapter:** a country's bonds are added as an adapter that supplies the products and the data sources. The engine and the screens stay the same. `docs/backend/bond-adapters.md` is a step-by-step guide for adding a country, including a mapping of US Treasury bills, I bonds and EE bonds onto the engine.
- **Downloads only on your request:** Monize downloads a series or a benchmark only after you add a bond, fetch a series or switch on automatic fetching.

**Want the bonds of your country?** Open a Discussion that names the bond types and where their terms and rates are published. Once the approach is agreed, a pull request is welcome, and the adapter guide shows what it has to contain.

### Polish retail treasury bonds

The first country on the new engine: all eight Polish retail treasury bonds (OTS, ROR, DOR, TOS, COI, EDO, ROS, ROD).

- **Adding a bond:** choose Type **Bond**, Country **Poland** and the bond kind (for example EDO). The kind fills in the symbol, the name, the currency and the issuer's page.
- **Series:** each purchase records its series, picked by code or by the month it was on sale. Terms come from the official issue letters on obligacjeskarbowe.pl.
- **Rates:** inflation from GUS and the NBP reference rate are fetched automatically. Inflation-indexed periods use the reading the issue letter names.
- **Valuation:** a daily value for every series you hold, with accrued interest and the early-redemption value.
- **Security page:** your series and lots, the current rate and the next rate change, a breakdown of the bond kind, a Terms history tab with every stored series, the issue letters under Documents, and a Refresh that shows its progress.
- **Redemptions:** when you record a redemption, the form suggests the returned principal and the interest. It also accounts for the early-redemption fee.
- **Notifications:** a new **Bonds** category in the notification settings tells you when:
  - a series matures within 14 days;
  - interest is paid;
  - a new interest period starts;
  - a matured series has no redemption booked;
  - a new inflation or NBP reading changes your rates;
  - a figure cannot be worked out, with what is missing and how to fix it.
- **Checked against the Ministry of Finance:** the engine's figures are tested against the Ministry's own published examples.

## Email receipts: purchases paid later

A "buy now, pay later" purchase used to leave you choosing between two incomplete readings. The shop's purchase email lists every item, but no bank transaction exists on its day. The provider's repayment email matches the bank transaction weeks later, but names only the first product of each purchase it repays. Monize can now join them.

- **Purchase and repayment profiles.** A profile can now be a **purchase profile** (its emails are stored with the key they share with the repayment, for example a contract number, and never look for a bank transaction) or a **repayment profile** (it matches the bank transaction as before, and reads each purchase it pays for as a part with that key). Other profiles work exactly as before.
- **One transaction, every item.** When the repayment arrives, each part is replaced by the lines of its purchase email, with the purchase profile's categories, shipping and discounts. A repayment covering three purchases becomes one split with every item of all three. A part whose purchase email is not stored stays one line, and the email says which purchases are still missing.
- **A purchase email that arrives later** updates the repayment's proposal, even after you approved the first one. You confirm the new split like any other.
- **Instalments.** A purchase paid in several repayments is split proportionally over them, to the cent, and Monize keeps track of how much of it is still to be paid.
- **Switching an existing purchase profile.** Make your old purchase profile a purchase profile and use **Re-parse emails**. Its emails are read again as purchases, and the repayment can take over a transaction that one of them had already enriched. A transaction enriched by an unrelated email is shown as a conflict, with that email named and the Link action to replace it, instead of being left unmatched without a reason.
- **Approval is always yours.** Auto-apply never applies a proposal that joins emails.
- **Drafting the profiles with AI.** Select up to 5 emails from each of up to 5 senders together, for example the shop's purchase emails and the provider's repayment emails, and choose **Prepare a profile with AI**. The assistant writes one profile per sender and can test the repayment profile against the unsaved purchase profile before you approve anything. A confirmation and short hints on the Emails and Profiles tabs explain when to use it: only when the facts of one bank transaction are spread over emails of several senders. Ticked emails now stay ticked when you pick another sender in the tag cloud, change the state filter or change the page.
- **Fixed:** an order email could match an income transaction of the same amount (a +249.00 income matched a 249.00 purchase). An order email now only matches an expense.

## Email receipts: acting on a selection

- **More actions on selected emails.** Besides delete and ignore, the selection bar can now **Reprocess**, **Restore ignored** and **Approve proposals** for every selected email. Reprocessing runs in steps with progress and a Stop button. Approval first says how many proposals it will approve and how many trade confirmations it leaves out, because a trade is approved on its own card in the review inbox.
- **Narrow a selection.** **Deselect by** takes emails out of a selection by state, by sender, or by received date (before or after a day). This works for the ticked rows and for "every email matching the filter", where the bar shows the server's count of what is left. That count is the number every action then acts on.
- **The AI assistant can read a saved profile in full.** When you ask it to revise a profile, it now reads the whole definition, not only the first 200 characters, and can save a new draft from it.

## Notifications for AI review proposals

An AI proposal in the review inbox expires 30 days after its request was raised, and until now nothing told you one was waiting. A new **AI review** category in the notification settings tells you when:

- new proposals wait for your approval (at most one notice an hour, however many proposals arrived);
- proposals expire in 3 days (one reminder per proposal, grouped into one notice a day).

The notices carry only the number of proposals and the expiry date, never the request's instruction, the payee, the amount or the email text. Each one opens the review inbox. As with every category, choose in-app, email or push in the notification settings.
## Fixes

- **"View email receipts" on an AI review request opens that email.** The link on a request raised from an order-confirmation email now opens the email itself, not the list of all emails. If the email was deleted, the link still opens the list.
- **Approve an AI review proposal in the chat when an MCP agent answers.** When the chat hands your prompt to a connected MCP agent and the agent proposes an edit for an AI review request, the chat now shows the proposal's card with its Approve button. Before, you could approve it only in the review inbox, which still shows it.
